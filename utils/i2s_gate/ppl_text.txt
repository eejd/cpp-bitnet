The water cycle describes how water moves between the oceans, the atmosphere and the land. Heat from the sun causes water to evaporate from the surface of the sea, from lakes and rivers, and from the leaves of plants. The vapour rises, cools, and condenses into tiny droplets that gather as clouds. When the droplets grow heavy enough they fall as rain, snow or hail. Some of this water runs over the ground into streams and returns to the sea, while some soaks into the soil and slowly travels underground. Plants draw part of it up through their roots and release it again to the air. Because the total amount of water on the planet changes very little, the same molecules are used again and again, sometimes spending only days in the air and sometimes thousands of years in deep ice or groundwater. Engineers who design dams, farms and cities must understand these flows, since a few unusually dry or wet seasons can change how much water a region can safely use.
